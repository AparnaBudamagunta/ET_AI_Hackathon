BUSINESS QUESTION THIS TABLE ANSWERS:
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
"Which products NEED to be promoted because we have too much stock sitting in the warehouse costing us money?"

Ans - Inventory is the first filter in our 9-step promotion planning process. Before the AI reasons about anything — we need to know what stock reality looks like.

Every Column
sku_id
──────────
WHAT IT IS:   Unique identifier for each product
BUSINESS USE: Links inventory to the product catalog
              (joins to products table to get name, price, category, geography)
EXAMPLE:      SKU_BEV_001 = Coca-Cola 2L India

current_stock
──────────
WHAT IT IS:   How many units are physically sitting in the warehouse right now
BUSINESS USE: Tells us the scale of the inventory problem or opportunity
EXAMPLE:      1840 units of Coca-Cola 2L

WHY IT MATTERS:
  Too high → money tied up in warehouse storage costs accumulating risk of expiry or obsolescence
             → PROMOTE to clear it

  Too low  → risk of stocking out during promo
             → DO NOT promote aggressively
             → promo would fail on day 1

target_days_cover
────────────────────
WHAT IT IS:   How many days of stock the business WANTS to hold at any given time
BUSINESS USE: The benchmark for what is healthy
EXAMPLE:      30 days — we want 30 day's worth of stock at all times

WHY 30 DAYS?
  Industry standard for most retail categories
  Enough buffer for supply chain delays
  Not so much that cash is tied up unnecessarily
  Retailer configures this in constraints.yaml
  Different categories can have different targets:
    Fresh produce:   7 days
    FMCG:           30 days
    Electronics:    45 days
    Apparel:        60 days

avg_daily_sales
────────────────────
WHAT IT IS:   How many units this product sells on an average day with no promotion
BUSINESS USE: The denominator in the most important calculation in inventory planning

EXAMPLE:      Coca-Cola 2L sells 20 units/day normally

WHY IT MATTERS:
  This is how we calculate days_cover:
  current_stock / avg_daily_sales = days_cover
  1840 / 20 = 92 days of stock

  Without this number:
  → We cannot tell if 1840 units is too much or too little
  → 1840 units for a product selling 200/day is only 9 days — dangerously low
  → 1840 units for a product selling 20/day is 92 days — dangerously high

  This is the context that makes stock meaningful.

days_cover (calculated)
────────────────────
WHAT IT IS:   current_stock / avg_daily_sales
BUSINESS USE: The single most important inventory metric
              "How many days will current stock last at normal sales rate?"

EXAMPLE:      1840 / 20 = 92 days cover

INTERPRETATION:
  < 15 days:  LOW STOCK    → do not promote risk of stocking out
  15-45 days: HEALTHY      → promote cautiously or don't promote
  > 45 days:  OVERSTOCKED → promote aggressively need to clear stock

stock_status (calculated)
────────────────────
WHAT IT IS:   OVERSTOCKED / HEALTHY / LOW_STOCK
BUSINESS USE: Simple flag for the AI engine

OVERSTOCKED:  days_cover > target × 1.5 (more than 45 days when target is 30)
              → Strong candidate for promotion

HEALTHY:      days_cover between 15 and 45 days
              → Promote only if other reasons exist (traffic driver, seasonal opportunity)

LOW_STOCK:    days_cover < 15 days
              → Hard block on promotion
              → Guardrail will reject this product

How This Table Is Used in Our AI Engine
────────────────────────────────────────
STEP 1 — Product Selection:
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
AI looks at inventory_status table
Filters to OVERSTOCKED products first
These are the primary candidates

Joins with products table to get: price, margin, category, geography

Scores each product:
  High days_cover  → higher promotion score
  High margin      → higher promotion score
  Right category   → higher promotion score
  Right geography  → higher promotion score

STEP 6 — Geographic customisation:
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Checks regional stock before recommending a promotion in a specific region

"Do we have enough stock in Indian warehouses to run this promotion in Mumbai and Delhi?"

If stock < expected promo volume → exclude region

STEP 9 — Simulation:
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
PySpark uses current_stock as the ceiling for maximum units that can be sold

Simulation cannot recommend selling more units than physically exist in stock

GUARDRAIL:
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
If a product has LOW_STOCK status → hard block → AI cannot recommend it regardless of how good the margin is

Real Business Example
────────────────────
SCENARIO:
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
SKU_STA_001 — Classmate Notebook India
  current_stock:     3,200 units
  avg_daily_sales:   40 units/day
  days_cover:        80 days
  target:            30 days
  status:            OVERSTOCKED

BUSINESS REALITY:
  We have 80 days of stock
  We only want 30 days
  We have 50 days of EXCESS stock
  = 50 × 40 = 2,000 excess units
  
  At ₹45 price → ₹90,000 tied up in excess stock
  Storage cost accumulating daily
  Back to school season ending soon
  → Stock will become even harder to move

AI DECISION:
  High promotion score - Recommend aggressive clearance promotion
  Before back-to-school season ends - Bundle with Reynolds Pen Pack (complement)
  Target price-sensitive segment
  Run in India only (that is where the stock is)

Why These Columns and Not Others
────────────────────────────────────────
WHAT WE DELIBERATELY LEFT OUT:
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Warehouse location    → v2 feature (regional warehouses)
                        For hackathon — assume one warehouse per geography

Expiry date           → relevant for perishables
                        our sample data is non-perishable
                        add in v2 for grocery retailers

Last reorder date     → supply chain feature
                        beyond hackathon scope

Shrinkage / damage    → operations feature
                        not needed for promotion planning

Seasonal adjustment   → handled by holiday calendar
                        not needed in inventory table
                        AI uses holidays config instead

RULE WE FOLLOWED:
  Only columns the AI engine actually uses in its 9-step reasoning process
  Nothing extra that adds complexity without adding decision-making value

Summary
────────────────────
INVENTORY TABLE PURPOSE:
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Answers: "What needs to be promoted and what cannot be promoted?"

Four columns tell the complete story:
  current_stock      → scale of the situation
  avg_daily_sales    → context for that scale
  target_days_cover  → what healthy looks like
  days_cover         → how far from healthy we are

One derived column acts as the AI filter:
  stock_status       → OVERSTOCKED / HEALTHY / LOW_STOCK

This is the first gate every product must pass before the AI even considers it for promotion.

Overstocked - When we have more stock in the inventory than required its overstock.
Low Stock -  When we have less stock in the inventory than required its lowstock.
    What is required - This is something we provide from target_days_cover in inventory table
    We need to have amount of stock when compared with avg daily sales happening less than or equal to target_days_cover
        CASE
            WHEN CAST(current_stock / avg_daily_sales AS INTEGER)
                 > target_days_cover * 1.5
            THEN 'OVERSTOCKED'
            WHEN CAST(current_stock / avg_daily_sales AS INTEGER)
                 < target_days_cover * 0.5
            THEN 'LOW_STOCK'
            ELSE 'HEALTHY'
        END as stock_status
