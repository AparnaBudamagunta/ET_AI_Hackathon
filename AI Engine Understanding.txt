Step 0 — Scope Parser
The Problem It Solves

WITHOUT SCOPE PARSER:
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
User types free text
System has no idea what category, geography, timing, or segment they mean
Cannot query Delta Lake without structured filters

WITH SCOPE PARSER:
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Free text → structured object
System knows exactly what to look for
Can query Delta Lake with precision

What It Does Step by Step
INPUT: "Build a promotion for beverages in India for Diwali"

INSIDE THE SCOPE PARSER:
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
1. Take the user's text as-is

2. Send to Groq LLM with this instruction:
   "Extract category, geography, event, time window from this text.
    Return as JSON only."

3. LLM returns:
   {
     "category": "beverages",
     "geography": "India",
     "event": "Diwali",
     "needs_clarification": false
   }

4. Pydantic checks:
   Is category a valid value?     ✅
   Is geography a valid value?    ✅
   Is the JSON structure correct? ✅

5. Look up Diwali in holiday_calendar table:
   start_date: 2026-11-06
   end_date:   2026-11-10
   multiplier: 2.8x

6. Final scope object:
   {
     "category":    "beverages",
     "geography":   "India",
     "event":       "Diwali",
     "start_date":  "2026-11-06",
     "end_date":    "2026-11-10",
     "multiplier":  2.8,
     "currency":    "INR"
   }

OUTPUT:
  Clean structured scope object
  Ready for Step 1 to use

What If Input Is Ambiguous
USER TYPES: "Build me a promotion"

SCOPE PARSER DETECTS: needs_clarification = true

SYSTEM RESPONDS:
"To build the best promotion plan, could you tell me:
 1. Which category? (beverages / stationery / electronics / apparel / fmcg / all)
 2. Which geography? (India / USA / Germany / all)
 3. Any specific timing or event in mind?"

THIS IS D3 DEPTH:
The system handles uncertainty gracefully instead of crashing or guessing wrong

Step 1 — Product Selection
The Problem It Solves

WITHOUT PRODUCT SELECTION:
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
We have 50 SKUs
Which ones do we promote?
Random choice = wasted budget
Wrong choice = promotes something already selling well or something we have no stock of

WITH PRODUCT SELECTION:
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
AI reads inventory, margins, competitor prices, holiday timing — and picks the products that will benefit most from promotion with the clearest business justification

What It Does Step by Step
INPUT: Scope object {category: beverages, geography: India, event: Diwali, multiplier: 2.8}

INSIDE PRODUCT SELECTION:
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
1. PySpark queries Delta Lake:
   SELECT p.*, i.days_cover, i.stock_status,
          i.current_stock, i.avg_daily_sales
   FROM products p
   JOIN inventory_status i ON p.sku_id = i.sku_id
   WHERE p.category = 'beverages'
   AND   p.geography = 'India'
   AND   i.stock_status = 'OVERSTOCKED'

   RESULT:
   SKU_BEV_001 Coca-Cola 2L    92 days  OVERSTOCKED
   SKU_BEV_002 Sprite 1L       61 days  OVERSTOCKED
   SKU_BEV_005 Minute Maid 1L  42 days  OVERSTOCKED

2. PySpark calculates margin for each:
   Coca-Cola:   (89-45)/89  = 49.4%
   Sprite:      (55-28)/55  = 49.1%
   Minute Maid: (99-54)/99  = 45.5%

3. PySpark gets competitor price gap:
   Coca-Cola vs DMart:  89-85 = +₹4 (we are higher)
   Sprite vs DMart:     55-52 = +₹3 (we are higher)

4. All this structured data goes into LLM prompt:
   "Here are overstocked beverages in India.
    Diwali is in 35 days with 2.8x demand uplift.
    Score and rank these for promotion.
    Consider: inventory urgency, margin,
    competitor gap, festival relevance."

5. LLM scores and reasons:
   Coca-Cola 2L:    Score 88/100
   Sprite 1L:       Score 82/100
   Minute Maid 1L:  Score 71/100

6. Pydantic validates output structure

7. Results stored in Delta Lake

OUTPUT:
  Top 3 ranked products with scores and reasoning
  Each product has a clear business justification

Why PySpark Does the Querying Not the LLM
WRONG APPROACH:
  Ask LLM: "Which beverages in India are overstocked?"
  LLM does not know — it cannot query databases
  It would guess or hallucinate

RIGHT APPROACH:
  PySpark queries Delta Lake → gets exact answer
  LLM receives the answer → reasons about it

PySpark knows the data.
LLM knows how to reason about data.
Never confuse their roles.

Step 2 — Mechanism Design
The Problem It Solves
WITHOUT MECHANISM DESIGN:
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
We always do a flat discount. Sometimes this works
Often, it destroys margin unnecessarily or it is the wrong tool for the segment

WITH MECHANISM DESIGN:
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
AI picks the RIGHT promotion tool for the RIGHT product and segment based on all available context

What It Does Step by Step
INPUT:
  Selected product: Coca-Cola 2L
  Margin: 49.4%
  Primary goal: inventory clearance (92 days cover)
  Segments: premium, mid_income, price_sensitive
  Competitor: DMart at ₹85 (we at ₹89)
  Complementary products available: Sprite 1L (also overstocked)
  Event: Diwali (home entertaining context)

INSIDE MECHANISM DESIGN:
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
LLM reasons:

"Goal is inventory clearance.
 Two products are overstocked: Coca-Cola + Sprite.
 Diwali context = home entertaining = people buy multiple beverages together.
 Bundle opportunity: clear two SKUs at once.
 Competitor DMart at ₹85 for Coca-Cola alone.
 Our bundle at ₹129 (₹89+₹55-₹15 saving) - gives better perceived value than DMart's single bottle at ₹85.
 Premium segment → no discount, loyalty points.
 Mid-income → bundle offer.
 Price-sensitive → bundle + extra ₹10 off."

OUTPUT:
  mechanism: BUNDLE
  primary: SKU_BEV_001 (Coca-Cola 2L)
  secondary: SKU_BEV_002 (Sprite 1L)
  bundle_saving: ₹15
  exception: premium segment gets loyalty points instead of bundle
  reasoning: full explanation

Step 3 — Discount Optimisation
The Problem It Solves
WITHOUT DISCOUNT OPTIMISATION:
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
We give the same discount to everyone
We either:
  Over-discount loyal customers who would have bought anyway (waste margin)
  OR
  Under-discount price-sensitive customers who needed a bigger nudge (miss the sale)

WITH DISCOUNT OPTIMISATION:
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Minimum effective discount per segment, no rupee of discount wasted, every segment gets exactly what it needs to trigger the purchase — nothing more

What It Does Step by Step
INPUT:
  Bundle price before discount:
  Coca-Cola ₹89 + Sprite ₹55 = ₹144
  Combined COGS: ₹45 + ₹28 = ₹73
  Min margin constraint: 20%
  Segments with price_sensitivity scores

PYSPARK CALCULATES THE FLOOR:
  At 20% margin: price = 73/0.80 = ₹91.25
  Round up: minimum bundle price = ₹92
  Maximum possible discount = ₹144 - ₹92 = ₹52

LLM DECIDES PER SEGMENT:
  Premium (sensitivity 0.2):
    Does not respond to discounts
    → Loyalty points (2x points on bundle)
    → No price change

  Mid-income (sensitivity 0.6):
    Responds moderately
    → ₹129 bundle (10% off)
    → Margin: (129-73)/129 = 43.4% ✅

  Price-sensitive (sensitivity 0.9):
    Needs a real deal to switch
    → ₹109 bundle (24% off)
    → Margin: (109-73)/109 = 33% ✅

  Lapsed (sensitivity 0.7):
    Needs reactivation
    → ₹99 bundle (31% off)
    → Margin: (99-73)/99 = 26.3% ✅

ALL ABOVE 20% CONSTRAINT ✅

OUTPUT:
  Discount per segment
  Resulting margin per segment
  Constraint status per segment

Step 4 — Cannibalization
The Problem It Solves
WITHOUT CANNIBALIZATION CHECK:
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
We celebrate Coca-Cola bundle selling 400 units
We do not notice Minute Maid lost 80 units
Net gain is only 320 units — not 400
We overestimate promotion value
We may have promoted at a net loss

WITH CANNIBALIZATION CHECK:
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
We see the full picture
Total category impact not just one product
We know if the promotion is truly worth it

What It Does Step by Step
INPUT:
  Promoted: Coca-Cola + Sprite bundle
  All other beverages in India:
    SKU_BEV_003 Tropicana Orange 1L
    SKU_BEV_004 Red Bull 250ml
    SKU_BEV_005 Minute Maid 1L

PYSPARK GETS BENCHMARK CANNIBALIZATION RATE:
  beverages India = 0.18
  (18% of gained sales come from own products)

LLM REASONS ABOUT EACH PRODUCT:
  Tropicana:
    Different brand (PepsiCo vs Coca-Cola)
    Different type (juice vs cola)
    Low switching risk → 5% cannibalization
    "Customers who want juice still want juice"

  Red Bull:
    Different type entirely (energy vs cola)
    Very low switching risk → 2% cannibalization
    "Different consumption occasion"

  Minute Maid:
    Same brand (Coca-Cola company)
    Same type (soft drink)
    Higher switching risk → 22% cannibalization
    "Most likely substitute for Coca-Cola"

PYSPARK CALCULATES NET IMPACT:
  Expected bundle sales: 350 units
  Minute Maid loss: 350 × 0.22 = 77 units
  Margin lost: 77 × (99-54) = ₹3,465

  Bundle gain: 350 × (129-73) = ₹19,600
  Net after cannibalization: 19,600 - 3,465 = ₹16,135 ✅

OUTPUT:
  At-risk products with cannibalization estimate
  Net incremental value after losses
  Risk level: LOW / MEDIUM / HIGH
  Recommendation: PROCEED
